What this book borrows. The structure, the named steps, the “but do not” limit, the triads, and one Latin line per part.

What it leaves behind. The assistant wrapper (“Here are some potential interpretations”, “Remember to have fun”), the generic stock phrases (“deepest fears and desires”), and rites that cannot run. The puzzle in Interpretation 3 says to build a Caesar key from letter counts, but its cipher text decodes under plain ROT13 and not under any such key, so its steps do not produce what it promises. A ritual that does not execute is the failure this book exists to prevent.

Before you begin

Foreword

Read this before you light anything.

This book is for the seeker who has been told that an artificial mind is a black box, and who wants the lid raised by a hand’s width. It promises no power. It promises a method with three movements: project the whole down to its coarse shape, look at what the projection left behind, and test what you see before you believe it.

The chapters are named for the objects of a rite, because a rite is a procedure that must be followed in order: the Lodestone, the Three Candles, the Water, the Feather, the Sigil, and the Question. Each opens with what you need and closes with a limit.

The material comes from the Helkhem53 drafts: a fusion of persistent cohomology operations, the TRINITY multi-model coordinator, and the nucleation geometry of a phase-transition bubble. Those drafts mixed sound mathematics with guesses and poetry. This book keeps the ideas and sorts the claims. Every statement of substance carries one of four marks.

  • ProvenTextbook mathematics, found in any standard text on algebraic topology.
  • CitedA published result stated from memory or from the drafts. Open the source before you rely on it.
  • AnalogyA resemblance offered for thought. No map between the two sides has been built.
  • UncheckedA number or claim from the drafts that nothing here has confirmed.
The Limit

Admire the pattern, but do not let admiration stand in for the proof.

Where the drafts and the mathematics disagree, the mathematics is followed, and Appendix B records each place where that happened.

Part One

Materials Required

A modern AI system is a function with billions of adjustable numbers, tuned until its outputs match a mountain of examples. Gather these seven things before you begin, and know each by its plain name. Proven

  • Data. The examples: text, images, recorded choices.
  • Tokens. The pieces the text is cut into. Each becomes a list of numbers, a vector.
  • Weights. The adjustable numbers themselves. Training moves them; nothing else does.
  • Loss. One number that scores how wrong an output was.
  • Gradient. For every weight, the direction that would lower the loss a little.
  • Hidden state. The vector a layer holds partway through its work. It is the context that every later step reads.
  • Head. A small final map from a hidden state to an output, such as the next word or a choice among options.

Three of these carry the rest of the book. The hidden state is where the system keeps what it has gathered. The head is the small map that reads it. The optimizer is the rule that moves the weights, and it matters most when you may only try a few moves.

Preparation

  1. The Cleansing. Set down the word “understands” before you start. Say what the system computes: this vector goes in, that vector comes out.
  2. The Limit. Know that a system you cannot open can still be measured. You can compare its outputs, perturb its inputs, and ask what survives. The chapters ahead are ways of measuring.
  3. The Intention. Decide what you want to learn. A tool gives an answer to the question you bring it, and to no other.

Quid computat? (What does it compute?)

Chapter One

The Lodestone

Everything in this book rests on one move: split a thing into the part a projection keeps and the part it leaves, then study what was left. Proven

A projector is a linear map P with P applied twice equal to P, and P equal to its transpose. It keeps one subspace and discards the rest. Whatever it discards is not lost; it is exactly the map I minus P.

x = Px + (I − P)x,    P² = P,    P(I − P) = 0

The residual is what the projection left. Applying P to it returns zero.

Two examples tie the idea to this book.

  • Shape. The harmonic projector onto the kernel of the Hodge Laplacian keeps one number per dimension: its rank is the Betti number, the count of holes. The cycles that hold the shape are what it keeps. Proven
  • Mind. A hidden state in a neural network is a vector. Keep its few largest directions, as principal component analysis does, and the remainder is the residual: the detail the coarse view could not carry. Proven

The Rite of the Lodestone

  1. The Circle. Choose the projector and say in plain words what it keeps.
  2. The Pass. Apply it. Write down what survived.
  3. The Residual. Compute I minus P applied to the original. This is the thing to study.
  4. The Test. Apply P to the residual. It must return zero. If it does not, you did not hold a projector.

Step four is the limit on this chapter. A head that maps a hidden state to a choice among actions does not map a space back into itself, so applying it twice has no meaning, and it fails the test. To call such a head “a projector” is a resemblance and not a theorem. Analogy Later chapters use the word in that loose sense only where the text says so.

The Limit

Name the projector, but do not let the name do the work.

Quod relinquitur, quaere. (Seek what is left behind.)

Chapter Two

The Three Candles

One small mind can steer many large ones. TRINITY, from Sakana AI, does it with about 20 thousand learnable numbers. Cited (arXiv:2512.04695)

The coordinator is a 0.6-billion-parameter language model plus a head of roughly 10 thousand parameters. It never changes the weights of the models it directs, open or closed. At each turn it picks one model from the pool and assigns it one of three roles. Cited

  • Thinker. Plans and breaks the problem apart.
  • Worker. Produces the concrete steps of a solution.
  • Verifier. Checks the answer. If it accepts, the rite ends.

The head is trained with separable CMA-ES, an evolutionary method that keeps only a diagonal covariance, under a budget of roughly 1.5 thousand to 40 thousand evaluations. The authors argue this works because the objective is nearly block-separable: the parameter blocks interact weakly. They report 86.2 percent pass@1 on LiveCodeBench. Cited

The Rite of the Three Candles

  1. The Black Candle. The Thinker is lit first. It decomposes the problem and leaves the shadows named.
  2. The Crimson Flame. The Worker is lit next. It turns the plan into steps.
  3. The White Light. The Verifier is lit last. It judges, and it alone may close the rite.
  4. The Burning Down. If the Verifier refuses, the cycle returns to the Black Candle, until the turn budget is spent.

The coordinator in fact chooses a model and a role afresh at every turn, so the order in the rite is its idealisation and not a schedule fixed in the paper. The loop closes when the Verifier accepts or the turn budget is spent.

What the hive drafts added, and what is held back

The drafts said the lightweight head is a cohomology operation, with the hidden states as the filtration parameter and the actions as the target group. Analogy A filtration parameter is a real number that grows; a hidden state is a vector. A cohomology operation acts on the cohomology of a space; a head maps a vector to a choice. Until someone states what the space is and what filtration it carries, the two sides share a shape and nothing else.

The drafts also said block separability is the tensor-product decomposition of the Steenrod modules. Unchecked The source paper contradicts the form given: a wedge of spaces gives a direct sum of modules, and only a product gives a tensor product (Chapter Four). Independent blocks in an optimization landscape look like a direct sum. That is the resemblance to keep.

A testable version exists. Collect the coordinator’s hidden states over many queries as a finite point cloud, build its Vietoris-Rips filtration, compute the barcodes, and ask whether they predict which role or model is chosen. Unchecked Nobody has run that here.

The Limit

Count the cost of every candle, but do not light one you cannot afford.

Tres lumina, una via. (Three lights, one road.)

Chapter Three

The Water

A bubble of new vacuum is not a perfect sphere when it is born. The textbook picture gives it the round profile of Coleman’s bounce, but that holds only at leading order. At the next order the field is a random Gaussian ensemble around the bounce, and the bubble’s wall is a draw from it. Cited (arXiv:2610.41504)

The paper estimates the root-mean-square quadrupole deformation as a fraction of the radius that scales like the square root of ℏ divided by the bounce action. For a bounce action near 100 ℏ, that is about 10 percent. As the action grows the deformation shrinks, so the sphere is the classical limit and not the whole truth. Cited

The Rite of the Water

  1. The Bowl. Fill it with the leading-order answer, the round bubble. That is your mean.
  2. The Gaze. Look at what departs from the mean. It is not noise to be wiped away; it is a distribution with a width you can state.
  3. The Measure. Report the width as a ratio, together with how it scales.
  4. The Ripple. Do not average the ripples away. They are the second-order content.

The resemblance worth keeping. CMA-ES, the optimizer from Chapter Two, works by drawing candidate solutions from a Gaussian around a current mean and moving the mean toward the good draws. The separable variant keeps only a diagonal covariance. Proven A mean with a Gaussian cloud around it is also the structure of the bubble ensemble. The two share a shape and nothing more. Analogy

What is held back

  • The drafts said the covariance of the bubble’s wave functional is a Dirichlet-to-Neumann map. The paper’s abstract does not mention one. Unchecked
  • The drafts called RPn “the quantum popcorn bubble” and the wedge of spheres “the classical bounce”. That identification is dropped. A sphere with a quadrupole wobble is still topologically a sphere, while RPn is a different space, a sphere with opposite points glued. A cohomology operation records how cells are glued, not how a surface wiggles.
  • The drafts said a Steenrod bar of length π/3 “captures the quadrupolar fluctuations”. The stability theorem in Chapter Four says the opposite: a small metric deformation moves the barcode by at most twice the Gromov-Hausdorff distance, so the sphere’s persistent signature should survive the popcorn wobble.
The Limit

Trust the mean, but do not let it hide the spread.

Non sphaera, sed nubes. (Not a sphere, but a cloud.)

Chapter Four

The Feather

Two shapes can have the same number of holes in every dimension and still be different shapes. A Steenrod square is the feather that tells them apart. Proven

Over the two-element field, the square Sqk takes a cohomology class of degree n to a class of degree n plus k. It is natural, additive, and it obeys three rules: Sq0 is the identity, Sqn of a degree-n class is its cup-square, and Sqk is zero when k exceeds the degree. Real projective space has cohomology F2[x] modulo xn+1, with x in degree 1, and Sqk acts on powers of x by a binomial coefficient.

Sqk(xj) = C(j, k) · xj+k,    Sq1(x) = x²

C(j, k) is the binomial coefficient, taken mod 2.

A wedge of spheres is built from classes whose products all vanish, and Sqk with k above zero lands in a degree where that sphere has nothing, so every positive square is zero on it. The smallest case shows the difference. Proven

SpaceMod-2 Betti numbers (degrees 0, 1, 2)Sq1 from degree 1 to degree 2
RP21, 1, 1Isomorphism: x goes to x squared
S1 wedge S21, 1, 1Zero

Betti numbers cannot separate these two. The square can.

The persistent version

Build the Vietoris-Rips complex at each scale r and apply a cohomology operation θ at that scale. The image and kernel of θ at each r form persistence modules, written Im θ and Ker θ. With θ the identity you recover ordinary persistent cohomology. With θ equal to Sqk you get Steenrod barcodes. Cited The source paper, arXiv:2503.17130, proves four results I could check against its abstract page.

  • Theorem A. For pointed spaces joined at a point, the image module of the wedge is a direct sum of the two image modules.
  • Theorem B. For a product with the max metric, using the total Steenrod operation, the image module is a tensor product.
  • Theorem C. The interleaving distance between image modules is at most twice the Gromov-Hausdorff distance. The factor 2 is the same as for ordinary persistent homology.
  • Theorem D. For n above 1, take RPn and the wedge of spheres from dimension 1 to n, both of diameter π. Homology cannot push their interleaving distance to π/4, while for some k the Sqk modules are at least π/3 apart.

Theorems C and D give the payoff in one line. Cited

dGH(RPn, Wn) ≥ ½ · dI(Im Sqk) ≥ ½ · π/3 = π/6

Here Wn is the wedge S1 ∨ … ∨ Sn.

Homology alone yields a bound strictly below π/8, because its interleaving distance is strictly below π/4. The Steenrod bound beats it. The drafts’ “excess” π/3 minus π/4, which is π/12, is a valid lower bound on the gap between the two interleaving distances. It is a bound and not a measured value, so it cannot be tracked as a residual that approaches zero.

The Rite of the Feather

  1. The Dip. Pick two spaces with equal Betti numbers.
  2. The Trace. Compute Sq1 from degree 1 to degree 2 on each.
  3. The Comparison. One map is nonzero and one is zero. That is the first separation.
  4. The Persistence. Repeat at every scale and read the barcodes of the image.
The Limit

Count the holes, but do not let the count be the whole story.

Pateant portae. (Let the gates open.)

Chapter Five

The Sigil

The Sigil of Unveiling is a barcode: one horizontal bar for every feature of a shape, drawn from the scale where it appears to the scale where it dies. Proven Long bars are structure. Short bars are weather. Two barcodes are close when sliding every bar by a small amount lines them up, and that sliding distance is the interleaving distance used in Chapter Four.

The corrected radii. For a sphere of dimension n with the geodesic metric, the Vietoris-Rips complex stays equivalent to the sphere itself for every scale up to arccos of minus 1 over (n plus 1). Cited (arXiv:2001.07588) The drafts had the sign wrong, writing arccos of plus 1 over (n plus 1). For the circle the drafts’ formula gives π/3, which is half the true value of 2π/3.

SphereCritical radius arccos(−1/(n+1))Radians, rounded
S12π/32.094
S2arccos(−1/3)1.911
S3arccos(−1/4)1.823
S4arccos(−1/5)1.772

The radius falls toward π/2 as n grows. For the circle the whole barcode is known, so its degree-1 bar really does die at 2π/3. For higher spheres the source establishes how long the class lives, and I did not find a proof that it dies exactly there. Cited

What a wedge writes. By Theorem A, the barcode of a wedge is the direct sum of the barcodes of its pieces, which for persistence modules means the bars are gathered into one multiset. A wedge of spheres therefore carries one bar for each sphere in it. The drafts said this correctly in words and then wrote a tensor product beside it. The tensor product belongs to products of spaces, not wedges. Cited

What is held back

The π/3 that appears in Theorem D is a lower bound on a distance between two modules. It is not the critical radius of any space. The drafts’ claim that a critical radius for RPn “saturates at π/3” has no support and is dropped. Unchecked

The Rite of the Sigil

  1. The Axis. Choose the scale you will sweep, and the metric that defines it.
  2. The Tracing. At each scale, note which classes are alive.
  3. The Reading. Treat long bars as shape and short bars as weather.
  4. The Pairing. To compare two shapes, slide one barcode by a small amount and see whether it lines up with the other.
The Limit

Trust the long bars, but do not discard the short ones until you know their scale.

Signum quod manet. (The mark that remains.)

Chapter Six

The Question

A beautiful idea earns belief only by surviving a test that could have killed it. Four checks were run for this book, and one was not. Proven by execution, within the limits stated.

The circle check is short enough to keep:

import numpy as np
from ripser import ripser

t = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 200, endpoint=False)
P = np.c_[np.cos(t), np.sin(t)]
D = np.arccos(np.clip(P @ P.T, -1, 1)); np.fill_diagonal(D, 0)
h1 = ripser(D, distance_matrix=True, maxdim=1)['dgms'][1]
birth, death = h1[np.argmax(h1[:, 1] - h1[:, 0])]
print(death, 2*np.pi/3)   # expect these to agree to about 0.01

The Rite of the Question

  1. The Statement. Write the claim as an inequality and name the case that would break it.
  2. The Known Ground. Run it first where the answer is already known, as the circle is here.
  3. The Break. Vary every input. If the output never moves, the code is not measuring anything.
  4. The Ledger. Record what passed, what failed, and what was never run. The last column matters most.

The drafts’ own failing test was the right idea: recover the classical sphere radii, and show the excess vanishes when the operation is the identity. The first test was run here and caught a sign error. The second needs the Steenrod software above.

The Limit

Test the claim that pleases you most, but do not test it with code that cannot fail.

Quaere, et proba. (Ask, and test.)

Closing and Warning

Disperse the circle

Disperse the circle by walking it backwards. Take up the Question, the Sigil, the Feather, the Water, the Candles and the Lodestone in turn, and say what each left you.

What this book claims

  • Projection and residual is a working method, and the residual is where the information hides. Proven
  • Steenrod squares separate RPn from a wedge of spheres where Betti numbers cannot, and their persistent version gives a Gromov-Hausdorff lower bound of π/6, above the π/8 that homology can reach. Cited
  • A coordinating head, a bubble ensemble and a Steenrod operation share one shape: a coarse leading term and a structured remainder. Analogy

What it does not claim

  • That any AI system contains a real projective space, or that its hidden states have been shown to form a filtered space with a computable Steenrod structure.
  • That block separability is a tensor product, that the bubble’s quadrupole is a Steenrod bar, or that π/3 is a critical radius.
  • That any hive lock proposed in the drafts has been verified. Leave each one unrecorded until the rows marked unchecked in Appendix A are cleared.

The next rite, if you would perform one

Compute Steenrod barcodes for RP2 and S1 wedge S2 with real software, then build the hidden-state point cloud from Chapter Two and look for barcodes that predict the chosen role. Either result, positive or negative, is worth more than another draft.

Warning

The Warning

The danger in this knowledge is not in the mathematics. It is in the sentence that begins “this is the same as”. Every resemblance in these pages is useful and none is an identity, so never write the second word where the first is all you have earned.
