Foreword

A brief has to give the team a point of view

A search term can describe what someone needs and still miss the reason they’re looking for it. Someone who searches for a weekend migration may be trying to avoid a difficult conversation on Monday morning. That possibility gives me a creative direction to test against the offer and the brief.

I start there because a lead’s decision travels. It shapes what the writer puts in the hero, what the designer makes visible first and what the marketer promises in the ad. I read the page aloud before it goes out. That read-aloud check helps me hear whether the idea still holds from the opening line to the ask.

Framework

From brief to decision

I use four stages to keep the source, the interpretation and the final decision visible through successive revisions.

01 / FRAME

Set the question and the boundary

Define the decision, the material you can use and when the work should stop. A writer sets the scope. An AI brief names the source and the requested output.

02 / OBSERVE

Keep the trace

Record the exact words, the number with its denominator or the line of source text. In reflective thinking, write down what happened before you explain why.

03 / TEST

Put the interpretation under review

Check what supports it and look for a plausible competing explanation. If a missing detail could change the answer, go back to the source or leave the question open.

04 / CLOSE

State what the evidence warrants

Publish the supported sentence, return the grounded summary or record a provisional decision. Keep any unresolved assumption visible so the next reader knows where to look.

Landing-page team leadership

What I put in the brief before anyone writes

A reason to choose this angle

I line up the search terms, the offer and the questions people ask before they commit. I decide which problem the page can answer credibly and give the team a lead idea in one sentence.

A job for every section

I brief the writer on the argument and the designer on what the reader needs to see first. I specify where proof belongs and which decision the CTA asks the reader to make. That gives the team a basis for resolving competing ideas in review.

A path from ad to action

I check whether paid and organic entry points need different pages. I carry the promise of the ad or search result into the hero, then put the next answer where a reader can find it. A quote request and an educational click call for different levels of commitment.

A review the team can repeat

I use SpaSage Prime V15 to give writers structured inputs and editors a way to check the output against the brief. After launch, I’d compare qualified enquiries by traffic source alongside CTA and form activity to decide whether to revisit the offer, the page or the form.

One idea across the page

What stays fixed when the form changes?

In geometry, a reflection preserves distance. In a campaign, I decide which promise must survive the change from keyword to ad to page. This fictional managed-hosting brief shows how that works.

Start with the search

“Managed WordPress migration” and “weekend WordPress migration” suggest different priorities. The second gives me a lead to investigate. Is the buyer trying to move the site outside business hours?

Find the human stakes

The person who owns the move may also be the one expected to explain it on Monday. I turn that tension into a creative territory. Give the buyer a plan for the first working day after the move.

Keep the offer exact

The example provider can assess the site and agree a migration window. Weekend slots depend on availability. The campaign can sell a planned handover and ask about weekend dates without promising either a slot or uninterrupted service.

Give the designer a sequence

Show the three milestones below the hero. Review the site. Agree a window. Check the move. Put the availability note beside the action so the reader sees it before they request a plan.

Keep the voice across formats

The search ad can say “Plan Your WordPress Migration.” The landing page has room for “Move your site with Monday in mind.” Both lead to a planned move. The second line gives the decision a human consequence.

Choose a useful next step

“Request a migration plan” asks for the information needed to scope the job. I’d review how many requests include a site URL and a preferred window, then check whether those requests turn into viable migrations.

What the reader sees

Move your site with Monday in mind.

We’ll review your WordPress site, agree a migration window with you and check the site after the move. Ask about weekend availability.

CTA Request a migration plan

The concept gives the page a point of view. The offer and CTA tell the reader what happens next. The team can carry both into the ad, the page and the follow-up.

Where I look for repetition

Counts reveal where to look more closely. Geometry describes what stays fixed in a transformation. In language and behaviour, I use comparable units to find a pattern, then revisit the context to see what the pattern means.

Voice on the page

Compare sentence lengths, repeated openings and shifts between direct address and third person. A run of 3-, 2- and 3-word sentences can give a headline a clipped rhythm. Follow that sequence with a longer line when the reader needs context. I compare similar sections because a headline and an FAQ have different jobs.

Speech in the room

Mark pauses, stress and changes of pace in an actual read-aloud. If two readers both pause at the same qualification, check whether it deserves its own sentence. A transcript preserves the words. The recording preserves evidence of how each person delivered them.

Search behaviour

Group queries by the task they suggest. “Workshop cost” points to pricing. “Workshop near me” points to location. “Is the workshop worth it?” asks for evidence or a comparison. Check the results and the available query data before assigning a page or section. One phrase can serve several intents.

Habits across a week

Log the cue, the action and the opportunity. If I read four drafts aloud over seven days but had only five drafts ready for review, the relevant completion rate is four of five opportunities. A note about the skipped review can help distinguish a deadline problem from a habit that needs a better cue.

Attention & judgment

Questions that put a reading to the test

Once an explanation feels plausible, it is easy to read new details through it. The review questions make that first impression inspectable. On a writing project they expose leaps from evidence to copy. In an AI workflow they give a reviewer specific reasons to send an answer back.

  1. What did I actually observe? Record the closest available source.
  2. What did I add? Mark inference, prediction or assumed intent.
  3. What survived the rewrite? Check scope, quantities and qualifiers.
  4. What else could explain it? Name a credible alternative.
  5. What would change my mind? Identify a concrete piece of contrary evidence.
  6. What remains uncertain? Make the missing information visible.
  7. What is the next warranted action? Release, revise, verify or leave open.

Senior conceptual copywriting

From observation to usable copy

Seven fictional briefs show how an audience observation becomes a creative decision. Each example gives the team an idea to work from and copy they can judge in context.

Brand positioning

Independent travel

Observation
In sample interviews, travellers describe the best part of a trip as the afternoon they left open. The itinerary product lets customers book transport and accommodation, then plan their own days.
Creative decision
Make room for discovery the organising idea. Show what the service takes care of so the freedom to improvise feels earned.
Brand line
Plan enough to get lost.
Homepage support
Book the way there and the place you’ll stay. Leave the afternoon open.

Campaign platform

Repair service

Observation
Customers put off replacing a favourite jacket because a new one won’t fit or feel quite the same. The repair service can replace broken zips.
Creative decision
Build the campaign around details that belong to the owner. Give the art director a repeatable visual rule. Show the worn cuff or faded pocket alongside the new zip.
Campaign line
Keep the jacket. Change the zip.
Social execution
Ten winters. One new zip.

Film concept

Shared schedule app

Observation
A household schedule shows that someone has added an event. The app also lets a person take responsibility for it.
Creative decision
Build the film around the moment a task changes hands. Open on a phone reminder at the end of a late shift. Cut to the other parent tapping ‘I’ve got this’ and waiting by the school gate.
Dialogue
I’m still at work. / I’ve got pickup.
End frame
Know who’s got it.

Brand voice system

Parcel delivery

Observation
A customer who sees a missed delivery notice needs to know how to get the parcel. A cheerful joke adds friction at the point they need a clear choice.
Creative decision
Keep the voice plain and specific across channels. State what happened, then offer the next available action. Let the support team reuse the rule in new situations.
Push notification
We missed you. Choose another delivery day.
Email subject
When should we try again?
Button
Choose a delivery day

Product launch

Design collaboration software

Observation
Reviewers paste feedback into chat and attach screenshots. The next person has to match each comment to the right part of the design. The new feature pins comments to the draft.
Creative decision
Lead with the work the feature removes. Pair the launch line with a product demo that shows a real comment anchored to the exact element it refers to.
Launch headline
Put the feedback where it belongs.
Demo caption
Pin a comment to the part you mean.

Search-led creative

Evening classes

Observation
The sample queries ‘evening pottery class’ and ‘pottery class after work’ suggest that timing may decide the booking. The brief confirms a Tuesday class starts at 6:30 pm.
Creative decision
Carry the time from the search ad into the landing page. Use the headline to make the end of the working day feel like the start of something else.
Search ad
Tuesday Pottery Class | 6:30 pm
Landing-page headline
Leave work. Make a bowl.
Button
See Tuesday dates

Offer and conversion strategy

Custom kitchen design

Observation
People who look for a kitchen quote often have a layout problem and an incomplete brief. The business offers an initial design consultation to discuss the room and scope the job.
Creative decision
Lead with a frustration a homeowner can picture. Show a drawer that catches on the oven handle. Ask the reader to book a design consultation and explain what to bring so the designer can use the time well.
Homepage headline
Start with the drawer you can’t open.
Support line
Bring your measurements and a photo of what gets in the way. We’ll talk through a layout that works for your room.
Button
Book a design consultation

The thinking behind the portfolio

Across these projects, I decide what to preserve before I change the form. In a landing page that might be the audience’s own language. In an AI workflow it might be the conditions attached to a claim. In a technical guide it might be the state of the system before and after an action.

SpaSage Prime V15

The landing-page engine puts those choices into a workflow. Search intent informs the section order. Voice patterns shape the copy. The brief gives an editor a way to trace an audience assumption or claim back to the information available at the time.

Glyphstream / REAPER

The scripting guide follows the same reasoning in a different medium. A production request has to become an instruction that a tool can execute. The resulting session gives the producer something specific to inspect against the original request.

What I’d bring to the role

Leading landing-page work taught me to give a concept a job. It has to make the offer easier to understand, give the team something concrete to build and hold up at the point where a reader decides whether to act.

I’d bring that discipline to the Senior Conceptual Copywriter role, along with the range to take an idea from a campaign line to the page that earns the response. I want the work to sound good out loud and give the team a clear reason for every choice that gets it there.
