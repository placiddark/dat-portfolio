Editorial Standards

A paid media landing page should answer three questions quickly. Is this the offer I clicked for? Is it relevant to me? What happens if I take the next step?

Carry the Promise From Ad to Page

Name the same service or offer on the first screen that the reader saw in the ad. If the ad offers a consultation, the headline and button should make that appointment clear.

Answer the Reader’s Next Question

Put the details people need to make a decision where they can find them. If a reader might wonder what a first visit includes, explain that before the page asks them to book.

Give Each Claim a Source

Check service details, credentials and testimonials against approved material. Refer any unresolved treatment claim to the appropriate reviewer before publication.

Make the Next Step Specific

Use a button label that describes what happens next. “Request a consultation” fits a form that sends an enquiry. “Book a consultation” fits a tool that lets the reader select an appointment.

Keep the Practice’s Voice

Use approved copy to understand how the practice speaks to patients. Keep that tone consistent from the headline to the booking form, especially when several people contribute to a page.

Make Open Questions Visible

When the ad and brief disagree, flag the discrepancy for a human reviewer. If the ad says the consultation is free and the brief gives no price, confirm the offer before the page goes live.

AIO Best Practices for LLMs

Clear answers help readers and make page content easier for search and AI tools to interpret. Start with sound SEO and apply these checks where they fit the page.

Name the Entities

Identify the practice, service and location in the visible copy. Make it clear which practice offers which service and where.

Lead With the Answer

Answer the question in the opening sentence, then add context. For example, “The first visit is a consultation to discuss treatment areas and preparation.” Use that wording only if it matches the brief.

Give Each Section a Clear Job

Use descriptive headings. Put common questions under their own headings and reserve lists for details readers need to scan.

Use Accurate Structured Data

Mark up the business and services where appropriate. Keep details such as hours, address and pricing consistent with the visible page. Google’s AI features require no special schema.

Make Facts Easy to Verify

Use approved figures and comparisons when they help a reader decide. Check credentials and references, and update details when the offer or service changes.

Make Local Details Useful

Name the city and service area when they matter. Explain what the practice offers in language that makes sense when read on its own.

Check Crawl Access

Check mobile performance, crawl permissions, canonical URLs and internal links. Review crawler settings by platform and check whether a tool supports llms.txt before relying on it.

Write in Plain English

Use short sentences where they help. Explain unfamiliar terms on first use and keep the precise terms readers need to understand the service.
